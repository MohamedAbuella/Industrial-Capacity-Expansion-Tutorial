Executive Summary & Core Insights

This tutorial models capacity planning and economic dispatch across three prospective biorefinery sites (A, B, and C) facing non-linear capital scaling (b = 0.70) and escalating carbon tax policies.

  • Scale Advantage (0–122.66 €/tCO₂): Site A dominates baseline economics (157.78 €/t) through economies of scale at its 120 kt/yr maximum size class and the lowest variable cost (90 €/t).
  • Carbon Tipping Points: Competitiveness transfers to Site B at 122.66 €/tCO₂, and to the lowest-emission facility (Site C) above 169.15 €/tCO₂.
  • Optimal 2020–2030 Path: Phased expansion at Site A (Medium → Large) coupled with an initial Small build at Site B minimizes the summed 2020 and 2030 annual system costs.

Capital Annualization Principle & Economic Scaling:
Annualization reconciles lumpy upfront capital expenditure with recurring operational expenditure by converting overnight investment into an equivalent annual fixed charge via the capital recovery factor (CRF = 10.19% at 8% WACC over 20 years) plus 4% fixed O&M, yielding f = 14.19%/yr. Site A achieves the lowest levelized production cost (157.78 EUR/t) because it combines the lowest variable cost (90 EUR/t) with the lowest unit fixed cost: deploying its maximum permitted capacity (120 kt/yr) exploits economies of scale (b = 0.70) to dilute unit fixed costs to 67.78 EUR/t. Conversely, Site C is restricted to 30 kt/yr, yielding an adverse unit fixed cost of 102.73 EUR/t and the highest total cost (230.73 EUR/t).

Carbon Price Crossover Insights:
Below 122.66 EUR/t CO2, Site A dominates on pure capital-scale efficiency despite being carbon-intensive (0.70 t CO2/t). In the intermediate bracket of 122.66 to 169.15 EUR/t CO2, Site B is least-cost, balancing moderate scale with lower carbon intensity. Above 169.15 EUR/t CO2, the scale advantage of large facilities is fully eroded by carbon liabilities, making Site C (the cleanest facility at 0.20 t CO2/t) the most cost-competitive option. Note that the A vs C crossover (145.91 EUR/t CO2) is not a regime boundary: at that price Site B is already cheaper than both A and C.

2030 Infrastructure Deficit & Average Cost Dynamics:
A single site cannot meet 2030 demand because the system-wide maximum permitted plant size is 120 kt/yr (Site A), yielding an effective output of only 102 kt/yr at 85% availability—short of the 110 kt/yr requirement. The system average production cost drops from 184.57 EUR/t in 2020 to 177.75 EUR/t in 2030 because capital-related cost per tonne falls from 94.57 to 86.66 EUR/t. In 2020 the Medium plant at Site A is only partly utilized (45 of 51 kt/yr effective, 88%). In 2030 Site A, upgraded to Large (102 kt/yr effective), runs at 100% and benefits from the stronger economies of scale of the larger class. Variable cost per tonne actually rises slightly (90.00 to 91.09 EUR/t) because 8 kt/yr must come from the more expensive Site B, whose Small plant is only about 31% utilized (8 of 25.5 kt/yr), which partly offsets the gain.

Mathematical Framework
Optimization Methodology & Mathematical Formulation
Non-linear power scaling, exhaustive enumeration of discrete build paths, and merit-order dispatch logic.
1. Non-Linear Overnight Capital Cost Scaling:
For any discrete capacity S, overnight capital cost is given by:
OCC(S) = OCC_ref * (S / S_ref)^b = 30 * (S / 60)^0.70 [MEUR]

2. Capital Recovery Factor (CRF) & Fixed Annual Charge:
With discount rate r = 8% and lifetime L = 20 years:
CRF = [r * (1 + r)^L] / [(1 + r)^L - 1] = 0.101852 [yr^-1]
f = CRF + FOM_pct = 0.101852 + 0.040000 = 0.141852 [yr^-1]

3. Effective Annual Production at 85% Availability:
Q_eff(S) = S * availability = S * 0.85 [kt product/year]
